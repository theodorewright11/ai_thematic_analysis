Below is a thematic analysis of the 20 public comments. I grouped both explicit requests and underlying sentiments. The themes are highly consistent across commenters, suggesting a strong shared perspective within this subset of ALS patients, caregivers, family members, and supporters.

# Major Themes

## 1. Urgency Due to the Rapidly Fatal Nature of ALS

**Most common theme**

Commenters repeatedly argue that ALS is a rapidly progressive, terminal disease and that regulatory processes do not adequately reflect this reality.

### Key ideas

* ALS patients do not have time to wait for traditional drug development timelines.
* Current approval processes are viewed as incompatible with a disease where survival is often measured in months or a few years.
* The guidance document is criticized for lacking urgency.

### Representative comments

D1, D2, D3, D4, D6, D8, D9, D10, D13, D15, D17, D19

### Illustrative statements

* "Treat ALS with a sense of urgency."
* "ALS patients don't have the luxury of time."
* "We cannot be having the same conversations 50 years from now."
* "The human cost is too great."

---

## 2. Expanded Access to Experimental Treatments

**One of the strongest and most frequent themes**

Many commenters advocate allowing patients access to investigational therapies much earlier, especially after basic safety has been demonstrated.

### Key ideas

* Access after Phase 1 safety studies.
* Expanded access/compassionate use.
* Allow patients to assume risk.
* Earlier treatment availability while efficacy data are still being collected.

### Representative comments

D3, D5, D7, D8, D9, D10, D12, D14, D16, D17, D18

### Underlying rationale

Patients are already facing a fatal prognosis; therefore, many commenters believe the risk-benefit calculation should differ from that of non-terminal illnesses.

### Illustrative statements

* "Give patients access now."
* "We need it now."
* "Allow people to choose what they would like to access."
* "The disease has already robbed them of the right to their health."

---

## 3. Opposition to Placebo-Controlled Trials

**Extremely prominent theme**

Many commenters view placebo use in ALS trials as unethical given the severity and fatality of the disease.

### Key ideas

* Placebos deny potentially beneficial treatment.
* Historical controls should replace placebo arms.
* Cross-over designs preferred.
* Ethical concerns regarding withholding treatment.

### Representative comments

D3, D4, D5, D7, D13, D14, D16, D18, D20

### Suggested alternatives

* Historical controls
* PRO-ACT database controls
* Single-arm studies
* Cross-over designs

### Illustrative statements

* "Placebo control trials should not be allowed."
* "Use historical controls instead."
* "If I'm going to risk trying a trial, I want the drug, not a placebo."

---

## 4. Demand for Innovative Clinical Trial Designs

**Core regulatory theme**

Commenters consistently criticize conventional ALS trial designs as outdated.

### Key ideas

* Traditional randomized controlled trials are seen as inefficient.
* Trial designs should reflect ALS biology and realities.
* Innovative methodologies should be encouraged rather than discouraged.

### Specific suggestions

* Historical controls
* Futility designs
* Predictive algorithms
* Data enrichment
* Lead-in data
* Single-arm trials
* Adaptive designs
* Cross-over studies

### Representative comments

D1, D4, D6, D7, D13, D17, D18

### Illustrative statements

* "Innovative trial designs should be the norm."
* "The trial design system is outdated."
* "The guidance discourages innovation."

---

## 5. Broader Clinical Trial Eligibility

**Frequently repeated operational concern**

Many commenters argue that current eligibility criteria exclude too many ALS patients.

### Key ideas

* Inclusion criteria are too restrictive.
* ALS heterogeneity is not adequately recognized.
* More patients should be allowed to participate.

### Representative comments

D2, D4, D11, D15, D20

### Specific concerns

* Only a small fraction of patients qualify.
* Slow-progressing patients are excluded.
* Narrow enrollment limits scientific learning.

### Illustrative statements

* "Widen the inclusion criteria."
* "Only 13% of ALS patients qualify."
* "ALL people living with ALS deserve a treatment."

---

## 6. Recognition of ALS Heterogeneity and Personalized Medicine

### Key ideas

### Representative comments

D4, D6, D17, D18, D19, D20

### Related concepts

* Genetic stratification
* Slow versus rapid progression
* Different treatment responses

### Illustrative statements

# Higher-Level Meta-Themes

Across all comments, five overarching narratives emerge:

### A. ALS Requires Exceptional Regulatory Treatment

Commenters argue that a rapidly fatal disease should not be regulated identically to chronic or nonfatal conditions.

### B. Access Should Be Prioritized Over Certainty

Many participants favor earlier access to potentially beneficial therapies even when evidence remains incomplete.

### C. Trial Modernization Is Urgently Needed

The dominant methodological recommendation is to replace conventional ALS trial models with innovative, adaptive, technology-enabled approaches.

### D. Patients Should Be Central Decision-Makers

Commenters consistently frame ALS patients as capable of understanding and accepting risk.

### E. The Current System Is Viewed as Ethically Inadequate

Many comments portray delay, placebo use, and limited access not merely as inefficiencies, but as moral and ethical failures given the fatal nature of ALS.
