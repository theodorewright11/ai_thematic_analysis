### MISUNDERSTOOD Subtheme: ALS community feels misunderstood by the FDA

*Subtheme of: Parent Theme: ALS community feels a significant relational deficit to the FDA*

### Definition

The ALS community (ALSC) lives in a world that is very different from the average human experience. People with ALS live with the knowledge that their days are numbered on the scale of single digits. This plays an immense psychological toll on what they value and how they go about living their lives. From various comments we see incisive prose directed at the FDA for failing to recognize the reality of their lives. Whether or not this truly reflects what the FDA understands about the ALSC, the comments show some perception and grounding that the FDA lacks some of this understanding since these comments are in response to a document representing the FDA’s thoughts around policy. This is most directly interpreted as misunderstanding rather than other possibilities of simple expressions of anger or frustration with reasons of the FDAs inability to make changes if they did understand. The comments look like the comments one would write to get ideas of understanding across even if emotionally charged.

### PRACTICAL CONSTRAINTS Subtheme: The ALS community has a hard time coming to terms with the idea that FDA systems and practices are subject to practical constraints.

*Subtheme of: Parent Theme: ALS community feels a significant relational deficit to the FDA*

### Definition

We see various expressions of emotion and backlash toward the FDA from the comments. One would reasonably assume that the FDA is not intentionally doing things to harm the ALSC. In other words, taking the story of the commenters at face value would paint the FDA in a light that contradicts their general role and track record in the world for trying to facilitate safer and improved drug development. One can reasonably assume that there are some real reasons why the FDA has made certain decisions based on its years of experience in their role. This is largely based on the asymmetric incentive structure of the FDA where approving a drug that later causes harm produces visible, attributable blame, while delaying or blocking a drug that would have helped produces invisible costs borne by patients. This asymmetry is also present in research norms and best practices where trial design conventions, statistical thresholds, and endpoint requirements were developed to protect credibility and scientific defensibility, not to optimize for patient timelines. The ALSC does not experience these as structural constraints, but as something that should not be in place, which has real weight given that they are nowhere close to the median environment the FDA has created norms around.
