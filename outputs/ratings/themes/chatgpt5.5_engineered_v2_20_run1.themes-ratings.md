### Reasoning

Across many comments, ALS is described as rapidly fatal and progressing faster than regulatory timelines. Commenters repeatedly contrast a 2–5 year life expectancy with drug development processes said to take 12–15 years or longer. The urgency is not simply a request for efficiency; it is framed as a fundamental mismatch between the disease and existing policy. Participants argue that conventional timelines are unacceptable because many patients will die before benefiting from approved therapies.

## Current evidentiary standards are viewed as ethically inappropriate for a terminal disease

### Definition

This theme captures objections to placebo use and to relying on death or survival as primary endpoints in ALS trials. Commenters argue that trial requirements should account for the ethical realities of a fatal disease and recognize meaningful benefits such as slowed progression, preserved function, and improved quality of life.

### Reasoning

Opposition to placebo-controlled trials appears repeatedly and is often tied to ethical arguments about terminal illness. Participants argue that withholding potentially beneficial treatment from dying patients is inappropriate. Closely related are objections to survival or death as endpoints, with commenters preferring measures of function, progression, breathing, swallowing, or quality of life. Together these concerns reflect dissatisfaction with how benefit and evidence are currently evaluated.
