They need to be given the option to take the drug, (and to thereby contribute to the body of research on its effectiveness) at the earliest possible date, without being subjected to blind placebo testing. The disease has already robbed them of the right to their health, it isn't necessary for the FDA to rob them of any possible hope.  It should be the role of the FDA to provide full data disclosure to the physician and the patient, then to assist the patient in considering the option to take the drug, and finally to carefully and monitor the patient's progress with the drug and collect all possible data on its efficacy.  The "lawyers" can assure that the FDA and the treating physicians are held harmless if the results are not successful.

[D18]
The draft guidance for ALS is completely unacceptable.  This document fails to recognize 1) the severity of this swift, fatal, and indiscriminate disease; 2) that it still lacks treatment; 3) ethical concerns of single-arm trials; and 4) that ALS is non-homogenous and therefore urgently requires newer, innovative trial designs to make possible the discovery of viable therapies.  Furthermore, this input from the patient community was received in guidance document meetings coordinated by ALSA - but removed from the published document.  The patient community has had numerous discussions with Dr. Janet Woodcock in which we received assurances that innovative trial designs are being embraced, and yet - this guidance actually DISCOURAGES THEM.   We were led to believe CDER was agreeable to utilizing data enrichment for innovative trial designs employing historical controls, lead-in data, and predictive algorithms to increase the efficiency of the trials, allow for single-arm trials, secondary endpoints, and crossover designs (placebo groups eventually receiving the treatment), and smaller trials.   Furthermore, ALSFRS-R, FVC, and SVC should be used as an endpoint - rather than survival.  Sponsors should be encouraged to make treatment available to participants who experience benefits during the trial.  This guidance is NOT a representation of the patient community. It is neither comparable nor remotely progressive contrasted with other recently released guidance documents for other diseases such as DMD and BCG-Unresponsive Nonmuscle Invasive Bladder Cancer - documents that recognize the ethical concerns and limitations of RCT.
